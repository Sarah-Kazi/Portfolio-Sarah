Hi. It’s 45 minutes to midnight, and it’s the 3rd of October tomorrow. 4 months since Jivi passed. I’m feeling too many things at once. I’ve been wanting to write about her for the longest time, but I could never get myself to sit and gather my thoughts or try to put my feelings into words. It was all too much, and still is. 4 months in and I still don’t know how to deal with grief, but I have gotten better at pretending otherwise.

Jivisha was my roommate for one entire year. I shared a room with her, Uditi and Tanvi in my 2nd year of college. Jivi was a junior from Delhi. She had taken a drop year, so she was the same age as us. The first day she came to our room, we were quite apprehensive about not vibing with her and shit. LMAO. She was a delight to be around, always full of energy, very respectful and the sweetest, kindest person ever. She became one of our closest friends and we had so many experiences together, so many firsts, so many inside jokes. Living with someone makes you dependent on them for a lot of things. You see their face first thing after waking up, you see them before going to sleep. I once had a funny dream about her, and after waking up I just looked at her and laughed for like 20 minutes straight. She was and still is THE BEST JUNIOR I’ve ever known. I have ~300 photos and videos of her in my gallery. Time just flew by in second year. Wish I could go back.

Anyway, after our final exams ended and 4th sem got over, she still had her exams going on because their batch started college a little late (she always ranted about how we never had exams at the same time). I wasn’t planning on staying in the hostel in my 3rd year, and neither was Uditi. We tried to convince Jivi too, but her parents were not allowing her. So Uditi and I vacated the hostel. I had an internship lined up at PIL for the next 2 months, so for the duration of the internship, I shifted to a PG close to PIL. Jivi, on the other hand, was preparing for her final exams in the hostel. Her grandfather was really sick and his condition kept getting worse. So much so that her father had completely lost hope. It was taking a toll on her exam prep as well cuz anything could happen anytime. Somehow, time passed and all but one exam was done for her, and that subject was kinda unimportant. It was a 2 credit subject (cie) and skipping it was an option. She was willing to give a backlog exam if needed, but she knew she wouldn’t fail. Randomly, we got a message from her saying:

<figure><img alt="" src="/blog/for-jivi/2.webp" loading="lazy" /></figure>

She had to go back to Delhi urgently and skip the last exam. She asked us before leaving if she should go and we said yes. SHOULD’VE STOPPED HER. Anyway, she went to Delhi. She’d been told that he had passed, but when she got there, she found out that he was still alive. He wouldn’t stay for long though. His condition was critical. Days went by, and we got busy with our lives for a bit.

It was the 3rd of June and I had a company visit to Akamai as part of my internship the next day, and we were instructed to wear formal clothes. I did not have formal clothes at all, so I decided to go shopping immediately. My mom had come to help me shift into the PG, so she came along. I went to the trial room and started trying on a few clothes, a little worried about not finding anything by tomorrow. That’s when I got a call from Uditi saying, “Bhai Joshika called me and told that Jivisha and her family died in a fire.” Joshika was Jivi’s friend. I froze. My first instinct was that it was prolly a prank and I did not take it that seriously. But then I found out it was Jivi’s uncle who had called and informed Joshika about the same. I started freaking out and calling Jivi. Her phone was switched off. Kept trying, repeatedly, but it was still switched off. I called Uditi, and while on call, we were trying to look for news articles about recent fire incidents in Delhi, and we found quite a few with her dad’s name (Vivek Aggarwal) in them. “There must be A LOT of Vivek Aggarwals,” we thought and continued to search. But then, we found one with her mom’s name in it (Tarjani Aggarwal) and then one with her nickname (Angel) and her sister’s nickname (Pearl). We were not ready to believe it at all. After a few hours, we found a lot of articles with their full names saying that all 8 members of the family lost their lives in the fire. They decided to stay in a hotel close to where her grandfather was admitted, and the fire broke out.

[https://share.google/sAVin3HYCLhWvm9pG](https://share.google/sAVin3HYCLhWvm9pG)

I read this article at least 10 times before I was ready to accept what had happened, all while still in the trial room. Thankfully, my mom was right there with me. I just told her that I was not feeling nice and had to get out of the store immediately. We left everything and went out, and I had a complete breakdown in front of her. I told her everything, sobbing uncontrollably. I couldn’t breathe. It came as a shock and I just wasn’t ready to accept that someone I was so used to having around me would just go away forever, somewhere idk. My mom took me back to the PG. I still couldn’t process it. We got Jivi’s uncle’s number from Joshika and I called him up. He decided to talk to my mom instead and told her everything. IT WAS REAL. SHIT. HOW. WHY. SHE DID NOT DESERVE THIS AT ALL. I was on call with Uditi and we were both crying. I didn’t know how to go back to being normal after this. I did not want people around me, but had to go for the Akamai visit the next day somehow. My mom kept telling me that it was fate, it was in a way good that she went and was with her family when it happened, which I did not agree with back then because I was being selfish. I just wanted her. But now I do agree, the survivor guilt would have been unbearable. It was almost as if death was waiting for her, scary to think of. Uditi and I calmed down after some time and started going through all of her pictures and videos, going back to every single memory we shared with her, and somehow fell asleep.

The next day, I woke up, hoping that this was a bad dream, but it wasn’t, it really wasn’t. I had informed a few people that I was working with, just so that someone knew, but I was not in the condition to do anything. I did not know how to hold my shit together in front of people, but I left for work. I received this text from my mom:

Sad but true. Had to go there and give an impromptu presentation about our project. It was a bad day. I did inform everyone who knew her about it. But it broke my heart seeing that they did not care as much as I did. They could just move on with their lives normally. She deserves more than just a casual “that’s tragic, RIP.” SHE DESERVES EVERYTHING. But later I realized that they just didn’t know her as well as I did. I miss her so much. I got through that day and the next few days somehow. It was too much to deal with.

I started putting people into 2 categories — people who would care and people who wouldn’t, and I completely stopped talking to the latter, which was not healthy. I did not feel like myself, couldn’t socialize, couldn’t talk to seniors or my own batchmates even. Having any kind of fun and feeling any joy just did not feel right. Felt guilty for feeling happy. Life kept going on, even if I didn’t want it to, so I had to get used to it. I got better at pretending over time. I still think about it at times. I don’t think one can ever forget someone. That void is very much still there. Random things remind me of her and my memories with her, and every time it hits the same. I’ve just learned how to ignore it better.

I miss her a lot. She was very important to me. I got the internship at PIL because she calmed me down before my interview. She was there for me whenever I needed her, even if she was busy. She had the kindest soul.

Her nickname was Angel. She WAS an angel.

I associate these songs with her.

Grief is something that I’m still figuring out, and I hope I get better some day.

This put so many things into perspective, honestly. It’s sad that we have VERY LITTLE time with everyone in our lives. We need to spend time with the right people, with people who care about us. Make sure that everyone in your life knows that you absolutely love them. Make every day with them count. Life is unpredictable asf, and it’s scary to think that someone you cannot imagine life without can just be gone on a random day. Go hug your friends and tell them that you love them and that they matter to you.

I hope Jivisha’s soul rests in peace. I hope she is happy, wherever she is. I hope she’s dancing and laughing.

I hope that wherever she is, she knows that she is loved deeply.
